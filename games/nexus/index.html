NEXUS

5v5 third-person shooter MOBA · PC (Epic Games) · 2022–2025 · Lead Game Designer

NEXUS is a 5v5 third-person shooter MOBA. Two teams fight to capture each other's Nexus, the source of one of the universe's most wondrous substances: Flow or Flux.

  • Designed the core mechanics, guns, abilities and items, and built the gun-balance spreadsheet.
  • Led balance through open alpha (Q3 2023) and full release with play-to-earn (2024).
  • Wrote the initial lore and theming, including the name NEXUS.

Design overview

NEXUS started when the company acquired an existing shooter MOBA. After testing it and doing early redesigns, we decided to rebuild the game ourselves in Unreal Engine 5, on Epic's then brand-new Lyra framework.

I wrote the initial design: mechanics, numbers, lore, theming and art direction. From there, most of my work was making three systems (guns, abilities and items) work together as a MOBA, then keeping a live PvP game fair once a flood of players (over a thousand) and real money arrived.

My rule throughout was to fix the cause, not the symptom. Early on, many "balance" problems were really missing mechanics, and no amount of band-aiding numbers would fix them.

I was the lead designer but not the only decision maker, so a big part of the job was presenting and explaining my designs to the other vision holders so we could debate them properly.

What I did

  • Lead Game Designer. Designed mechanics and documented them for the other vision holders, then polished them for the devs to implement. I explained the reasoning behind every design so it could be debated, because some final calls weren't mine.
  • Documentation. GDD (Google Docs from mid 2022, then GitBook, through mid 2025). A gun spreadsheet holding every gun's numbers, so non-devs could read and tweak them.
  • Research. Predecessor, Paragon: Overprime and Destiny 2 for gun and ability feel. Valorant and other hero shooters for abilities. League of Legends, Dota 2 and Heroes of the Storm to validate MOBA mechanics.
  • Balance and exploit-hunting. Combed through proposed changes for knock-on effects before they shipped. When exploits got through anyway, reverted or fixed them fast, and worked on buffing underused strategies without creating new problems.
  • Vision (shared). Wrote the initial lore and theming (which gave us the name NEXUS) and the initial art direction, then updated the lore and art direction for the map, structures, abilities and items in 2024.

Problem 1

Live exploits

The problem: However much you test internally, the moment a PvP game launches, players find things you never planned for. And once play-to-earn launched it got more intense: players behave very differently when money is involved.

ExploitWhy it happenedFix
Healing grenades enabled endless pushesThey healed minions tooHalved the healing they did to minions.
A 5-stack pushing mid could end the game in 5 minutesTowers were far too weak against a full-team pushGave players reasons not to group up. Adding levels and XP sharing helped greatly.
Grenade launchers dominated farmingSplash damage cleared minion waves far too easilyMoved them to a support role when we reworked guns: less damage, more utility.
Hugging your tower was too strongCaused by increasing the healing towers gaveSlightly reduced the healing towers gave. Compensated with 'homeguard', so backing wasn't punished, and with base regen for players.

Takeaway Every fix becomes a new system that players will push to its limit. Ship fixes fast, then watch what players do with them.

Problem 2

Power scaling and the economy

The problem: Player power across a match was unpredictable and all over the place.

  • Guns, abilities and items all cost money to buy and upgrade, so gold was stretched three ways.
  • Base stats like health and energy never grew unless you bought items for them, when really they should grow as you play a match.
  • In lane, poke was far too strong (everyone has a gun), so you spent the laning phase hiding behind your tower.
The iterations
  1. Regeneration around the base of your tower. It eased laning, but it only treated a symptom.
    • It also caused the tower-hugging exploit above, where it was almost impossible to kill players under their tower.
  2. Levels. Players now gain base stats "for free" as the match goes on, so power grows predictably and gold goes further. It helped a ton.
    • Levels also replaced buying abilities and their upgrades. That streamlined the game, left more gold for everything else, and delivered the 'you get stronger as the match goes on' fantasy we'd been missing. Three birds, one stone!

Takeaway When several problems trace back to a single system, fix the system, not each problem.

Problem 3

Roles and build diversity

The problem: Guns, abilities and items weren't exciting to buy, upgrade, use or play against. Some were overpowered (the grenade launcher farmed like nothing else), others were useless (the healing gun just didn't do enough). Together they left no real tank or support playstyle. You could play one, but it was so unfun that nobody did, unless it was busted.

The iterations
  1. The starting point. Tanks and supports existed on paper, but nobody played them. Items had unlimited slots and were simple stat buffs.
  2. Huge buffs. They became overpowered and had to be tuned back, and they still didn't feel good to play. More numbers couldn't make up for missing tools or uninteresting effects.
  3. More abilities and ultimates, and limited item slots. We launched with only three ultimates, so many playstyles were stuck with a mediocre one. Adding more helped a lot.
    • Limiting item slots added creativity. We could make items much stronger, and having limited slots to spend on what you needed in a given game felt much better. It also capped how far ahead someone could get, as they couldn't just keep buying items.
  4. New gun passives, new guns and new item effects, built around tank and support play. For example, the shotgun became a proper tank gun that slowed and granted max HP, and the healing gun gave a small damage buff to allies it healed and a damage-dealt debuff to enemies it hit.
    • With limited slots in place, and players used to the new items and economy, we had room to properly buff items. Every item got new effects and stats, so none was just "buff a single stat" any more.

A bigger item update was planned: moving items to a proper component system like most MOBAs, instead of the simple Rank 1/2/3 upgrades everything had to be balanced around. I was designing it when I moved to other games and development was paused.
